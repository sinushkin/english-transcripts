# Dialog 10: Parent-teacher meeting

**Level:** B2 · **Setting:** Mrs. Clark (teacher) talks to Ben's mother  
**Length:** 306 words, about 2.4 min spoken · **Idioms:** 16

## Dialogue

**Mrs. Clark:** Thank you for coming. I'd like to talk about Ben. In general, he's a bright boy, but I have a few concerns.  
**Mother:** Please go ahead, and don't **beat around the bush**. I'd rather hear everything directly.  
**Mrs. Clark:** Well, in maths he **falls short** of his real ability. He understands more than his grades show.  
**Mother:** Maybe he doesn't **get the point** of some topics. He told me fractions are "just boring".  
**Mrs. Clark:** It's possible. I try to **get** the main idea **across** with real examples, like cooking, but not every child reacts.  
**Mother:** At home I try to **put in** an hour of homework with him every day, but he **holds back** and says he's tired.  
**Mrs. Clark:** I see. There's another thing. Ben tends to **keep to himself** during breaks. He sits alone with a book.  
**Mother:** He was always shy. When he was five, he wouldn't even **mingle with** the kids at the playground.  
**Mrs. Clark:** Has he ever mentioned someone who **gets on his nerves**? I want to be sure nobody **puts** him **down**.  
**Mother:** Not that I know of. But I'll ask him tonight. If something happens, I'll **get back to** you.  
**Mrs. Clark:** Please do. I **take** such things very **seriously**. A child should feel safe at school.  
**Mother:** Thank you. Do you think he should **take up** some activity? Maybe a sport or a club?  
**Mrs. Clark:** I'd suggest the chess club. It meets on Wednesdays, and it's a quiet place where children **socialize** naturally.  
**Mother:** That's a great idea. Ben loves chess. I'm surprised it **slipped my mind**.  
**Mrs. Clark:** And one small step at a time. Let him **overcome his fears** slowly. Don't push too hard.  
**Mother:** I'll **take my time**, I promise. Is there anything else I should do?  
**Mrs. Clark:** Just praise him when he tries. Children like Ben need to feel that effort is noticed.  
**Mother:** Thank you, Mrs. Clark. I feel much better after this conversation.
